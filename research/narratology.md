# What keeps a viewer watching: narratology, film theory, screenwriting craft

Survey for the Snip0 Learn curriculum: narrative theory, cognitive film theory, screenwriting/TV craft, non-narrative holds. Psychology and platform research are sibling surveys. Every entry was checked against a web source (Sources); unconfirmed items are marked UNVERIFIED. No naming conclusion is drawn here.

## 1. Theories

| Author | Year | Work | Term(s) the author uses | What it explains | What it does not |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gustav Freytag | 1863 | *Die Technik des Dramas* | "pyramid": exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, dénouement | The tension curve of a play; the Cycle layer at whole-work scale | A shape, not a mechanism |
| Francisque Sarcey / William Archer | 1870s–90s / 1912 | Sarcey's theatre criticism; Archer, *Play-Making: A Manual of Craftsmanship* | "scène à faire" = "obligatory scene": "one which, for one reason or another, an audience expects" | Names the thing the audience has been promised and waits for; the anticipation "runs round the whole theatre" when the two parties finally face each other | Only the climactic, promised scene; no account of moment-to-moment hold |
| Alfred Hitchcock (with Truffaut) | 1962 interviews, book 1966 | *Hitchcock/Truffaut* | "surprise" vs "suspense": the bomb under the table, "fifteen seconds of surprise" vs "fifteen minutes of suspense" | Viewer knowledge is the hold: tell them about the bomb and a banal scene cannot be left | An aphorism; no place for curiosity |
| Meir Sternberg | 1978; 1992; 2003 | *Expositional Modes and Temporal Ordering in Fiction* (Johns Hopkins UP); "Telling in Time (II): Chronology, Teleology, Narrativity", *Poetics Today* 13(3); "Universals of Narrative and Their Cognitivist Fortunes" (I, II), *Poetics Today* 24(2), 24(3) | "suspense, curiosity, surprise" = "the dynamics of prospection, retrospection, and recognition"; "narrativity as the play of suspense/curiosity/surprise between represented and communicative time"; narrative is "a discourse where such play dominates"; "gaps" (temporary/permanent), "retardation" | The strongest published claim that these three interests are what makes something narrative at all; all three come from the gap between the order events happened and the order they are told | Everything is *information order*; nothing on spectacle, rhythm, or presence |
| Richard Dyer | 1977 | "Entertainment and Utopia", *Movie* 24 | musical numbers work through "non-representational signs" (colour, movement, rhythm, melody, camerawork); five "utopian sensibilities": energy, abundance, intensity, transparency, community, each answering a felt lack (exhaustion, scarcity, dreariness, manipulation, fragmentation) | Why a number holds when the plot stops: it delivers a feeling, not information | Not a theory of attention over time |
| Michael Fried | 1980 | *Absorption and Theatricality* (painting) | "absorption" (figures unaware of being watched) vs "theatricality" (the work addresses the beholder) | A vocabulary for the "presence" problem: watching someone absorbed in a task vs a performer who acknowledges you | About painting; imported into film/performance by others |
| William Brewer & Edward Lichtenstein | 1982 | "Stories are to entertain: A structural-affect theory of stories", *Journal of Pragmatics* 6 | "structural-affect theory"; three "discourse structures": suspense (outcome postponed), curiosity (outcome shown before its causes), surprise (unexpected event) | A story is enjoyed only when its discourse structure produces one of these three affects, resolved at the end | Short texts; no non-story holds; the "no three affects, no story" claim is contested |
| David Bordwell | 1985 | *Narration in the Fiction Film* (U. Wisconsin Press), Part 2 "The Viewer's Activity" | "the viewer's activity"; "hypotheses"; "gaps"; "retardation"; "primacy effect" (types in §2); builds on Sternberg | The viewer is always guessing; narration is the regulated flow of story information that keeps hypotheses open | Formal; emotion bracketed |
| Noël Carroll | 1985; 1990; 1996; 2001 | "The Power of Movies", *Daedalus* 114(4); *The Philosophy of Horror*; "The Paradox of Suspense" in Vorderer et al.; "On the Narrative Connection" in *Beyond Aesthetics* | "erotetic narration": earlier scenes raise questions, later scenes answer them; "narrative connection"; suspense = two outcomes, the morally right one improbable and the bad one likely; suspense works on "entertained uncertainty", so it survives rewatching | The most direct question/answer model of a plot, plus a moral-probability definition of suspense | Struggles where no question is posed (sport, dance, process); critiqued in *Projections* 14(1), 2020 |
| Tom Gunning | 1986; 1989 | "The Cinema of Attractions", *Wide Angle* 8(3–4); "An Aesthetic of Astonishment", *Art and Text* 34 | "cinema of attractions"; "exhibitionist cinema"; "attraction"; "astonishment"; the "direct address of the audience" by a "showman" | Pre-1906 film held viewers by showing, not telling; the attraction survives inside musicals, action, spectacle | A historical thesis; "attraction" is a catch-all |
| Rick Altman | 1987 | *The American Film Musical* (Indiana UP) | "dual-focus narrative"; the "seemingly gratuitous song-and-dance number" carries the film's structure | Numbers are not pauses in the story; they are the story's structure by contrast/parallel | Genre-specific |
| Richard Gerrig | 1993 | *Experiencing Narrative Worlds* (Yale UP) | "anomalous suspense" (we feel suspense on rewatching); "transportation" (with Green & Brock 2000, sibling survey) | Suspense is not literal uncertainty about the outcome | Psychology of reading, covered by the sibling survey |
| Dolf Zillmann | 1996 (theory 1970s–) | "The psychology of suspense in dramatic exposition", in Vorderer, Wulff & Friedrichsen (eds.), *Suspense: Conceptualizations, Theoretical Analyses, and Empirical Explorations* (Erlbaum) | "affective disposition theory"; suspense as "empathic distress" for a liked protagonist whose good outcome is unlikely; "excitation transfer" into the relief | Why we care who wins: liking + threat; why the payoff spikes | Needs a liked agent in danger; silent on curiosity, process, spectacle |
| Hans J. Wulff | 1996 | "Suspense and the influence of cataphora on viewers' expectations", same volume, pp. 1–17 | "cataphora": forward-pointing cues, "constructions of attention" | Names the *device* that opens an expectation (the lit fuse, the glance at the door) | Film-semiotic; little empirical follow-up |
| Ed S. Tan | 1994; 1996 | "Film-induced affect as a witness emotion", *Poetics* 23; *Emotion and the Structure of Narrative Film: Film as an Emotion Machine* (Erlbaum) | "interest" as the central, "centripetal" film emotion: "the urge to watch and actively anticipate further developments in the expectation of a reward"; film narration "manipulates the element of promise"; "witness emotion"; F-emotions (fiction) vs A-emotions (artefact) | The one theory whose core term is the hold itself: interest = an investment expecting return, renewed scene by scene | Narrative fiction film only |
| Marie-Laure Ryan | 2001 | *Narrative as Virtual Reality* (Johns Hopkins UP) | "temporal immersion"; "what suspense", "how (why) suspense", "who suspense", "metasuspense" | A typology of what exactly the viewer is uncertain about, including uncertainty about how the *author* will land it | Literary/game-oriented |
| Christopher Booker | 2004 | *The Seven Basic Plots* (Continuum) | seven plots (listed in §2) | A catalogue of arc shapes | Jungian; not about attention |
| Robert McKee | 1997 | *Story* (ReganBooks) | "the gap" "between expectation and result"; a scene where the protagonist gets exactly what they expect "is a scene not worth shooting" | The beat-level engine: action, then a result that differs from expectation, nested from beat to act | "Gap" here is expectation vs outcome, not withheld information (Sternberg's "gap") |
| Blake Snyder | 2005 | *Save the Cat!* | "Fun and Games" = "the promise of the premise", "why people bought a ticket" | The craft name for the section that delivers what the format promised | Feature beat sheet |
| Pamela Douglas | 2005 | *Writing the TV Drama Series* (Michael Wiese) | "teaser", "act break"/"act out", "tag", four-act hour, "cliffhanger" | Every act ends on an open question sized to survive a break; the cliffhanger is the pause-proof version | "Cliffhanger" predates TV (Hardy's serialised *A Pair of Blue Eyes*, 1873; the word from c. 1931) |
| Raphaël Baroni | 2007 | *La tension narrative: Suspense, curiosité et surprise* (Seuil) | "tension narrative": "the combination of uncertainty and anticipation"; same three kinds as Sternberg | Francophone synthesis; tension is the reader's *state*, suspense/curiosity/surprise its varieties | Untranslated |
| Reagan, Mitchell, Kiley, Danforth & Dodds | 2016 | "The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes", *EPJ Data Science* 5:31 | six arcs: "rags to riches" (rise), "riches to rags" (fall), "man in a hole" (fall-rise), "Icarus" (rise-fall), "Cinderella" (rise-fall-rise), "Oedipus" (fall-rise-fall); after Vonnegut's "shapes of stories" (rejected Chicago MA thesis; lecture; *Palm Sunday*, 1981) | Sentiment-over-time shapes of 1,327 Gutenberg novels | Measures valence, not attention or drop-off |
| Salomé Aguilera Skvirsky | 2020 | *The Process Genre: Cinema and the Aesthetic of Labor* (Duke UP; SCMS Best First Book 2021) | "the process genre": "the sequentially ordered representation of someone making or doing something"; "processual representation"; "a surprising degree of absorption" | The first genre theory of how-it's-made, cooking, craft, step sequences; the hold is a known end product plus unknown steps ("what is this?", "how does it work?") | Declines to theorise unboxing/TikTok ("I don't know... what do you think?"); Smucker (2022) says the hold is ingenuity, not dexterity |

Empirical tests of structural-affect theory: Hoeken & van Vliet 2000, *Poetics* 27; Knobloch, Patzig, Mende & Hastall 2004, "Affective News", *Communication Research* 31(3) (linear order gives more suspense, "reversal" order more curiosity, in news and novel framings alike); Bermejo-Berros, López-Díez & Gil Martínez 2022, *Poetics* 93 (the same three structures induce "narrative tension" in a video story, N=94).

## 2. Published taxonomies of kinds

Verbatim categories, with the working-kind they most resemble in brackets (my mapping, not the author's).

**Sternberg 1978/1992, "narrative universals":** suspense (prospection: future), curiosity (retrospection: past), surprise (recognition: a past you thought you knew). [suspense ≈ arc/process; curiosity ≈ reveal; surprise has no working kind]

**Brewer & Lichtenstein 1982, "discourse structures":** suspense, curiosity, surprise. Same three, defined by ordering: outcome postponed / outcome before causes / unexpected event.

**Ryan 2001, kinds of suspense:** "what suspense" (what will happen / what will they do), "how (why) suspense" (how did this come to be), "who suspense" (who did it), "metasuspense" (how will the author tie it together). [what ≈ arc; how ≈ process/reveal; who ≈ reveal; meta ≈ pattern, the viewer betting on the maker's craft]

**Bordwell 1985, hypothesis and gap types:** hypotheses are curiosity or suspense; exclusive or nonexclusive; probable or improbable; simultaneous or successive. Gaps are temporary or permanent; diffuse or focused; flaunted or suppressed.

**Carroll 1985/1990:** macro-question (the whole film's question) vs micro-questions (scene-level) UNVERIFIED as exact wording; the erotetic model itself is verified.

**Tan 1996 (from the book's chapter titles):** interest sustained by "thematic structures" and by "character structures, empathy"; and F-emotions (about the fiction) vs A-emotions (about the artefact, i.e. admiring the making). [A-emotions are the closest published home for "presence"/watching skill]

**Kintsch 1980:** "cognitive interest" (unexpected information) vs "emotional interest" (arousing topics); reveal is the first, arc the second.

**Dyer 1977, utopian sensibilities:** energy, abundance, intensity, transparency, community. [the only published taxonomy of what a *number* delivers rather than what it withholds]

**Vonnegut / Reagan et al. 2016, arc shapes:** rise; fall; fall-rise (man in a hole); rise-fall (Icarus); rise-fall-rise (Cinderella); fall-rise-fall (Oedipus). **Booker 2004:** Overcoming the Monster; Rags to Riches; The Quest; Voyage and Return; Comedy; Tragedy; Rebirth.

No published taxonomy has four kinds matching arc / process / reveal / pattern. The recurring split is two-way on *time* (future-facing suspense vs past-facing curiosity) plus surprise as the disruptor. "Process" is a genre (Skvirsky), not a kind of interest; in her account it runs on the same three once the end product is known. "Pattern" (will the next beat land) has no narratological name; nearest are Ryan's "metasuspense" and Tan's A-emotions.

## 4. Holding without story

**Spectacle.** Gunning (1986, 1989): early film was "exhibitionist"; it held by "attraction" and "astonishment", addressing the viewer and showing something, no plot required; the attraction survives inside narrative cinema as the number, the stunt, the effect. Bordwell (*Planet Hong Kong*, 2000) adds a mechanism for action: "expressive amplification" and a "pause-burst-pause" rhythm, legible gesture punctuated so each burst is set up and lands. That is a pattern hold, not an arc.

**The number.** Dyer (1977): a number delivers energy, abundance, intensity, transparency, community through non-representational means; the viewer stays for a *feeling*, not an answer. Altman (1987): the "gratuitous" number is the structure. Both say the story can stop and the hold continues.

**Process.** Skvirsky (2020): the process genre holds through "absorption"; a known end product plus unknown steps, so it is narrative-shaped (she connects it to "surprise, suspense, and curiosity") even though no one changes. Smucker's amendment: what holds is the visible ingenuity of hands and tools. This supports "process = will it finish" as its own kind, with the caveat that it may be suspense with a product in place of a person.

**Performance / presence.** Fried (1980): "absorption" (the performer does not know you are there) vs "theatricality" (they address you). Gunning's direct address is theatricality; a craftsman filmed at work is absorption. Dance studies call the hold "kinesthetic empathy" (John Martin, 1930s; Reynolds & Reason, eds., Intellect, 2012): the spectator moves with the mover. Tan's A-emotions (admiring the artefact and its maker) are the film-theory home for watching skill. None of these need an open question; they are the literature's answer that story is not required.

**Loops.** Manovich, *The Language of New Media* (MIT Press, 2001), has a chapter "Loop as a Narrative Engine" but offers no reception theory. Nothing in this slice explains ASMR or seamless loops.

**Where the traditions agree.** Two hold mechanisms are published: (a) an open expectation about information (Sternberg, Carroll, Bordwell, Brewer & Lichtenstein, Baroni); (b) delivered sensation with nothing withheld (Gunning, Dyer, Fried, Martin). Tan's "interest as expectation of reward" is the only term spanning both, since the reward can be an answer or a feeling.

### 2. 公刊された種類の分類

原文どおりのカテゴリーと、それが最も近い working-kind（作業上の種類）を角括弧に示す（著者ではなく私の対応づけ）。

**Sternberg 1978/1992, 「narrative universals（物語の普遍項）」：** suspense（prospection：未来）、curiosity（retrospection：過去）、surprise（recognition：知っていると思っていた過去）。[suspense ≈ arc/process; curiosity ≈ reveal; surprise には対応する working kind がない]

**Brewer & Lichtenstein 1982, 「discourse structures」：** suspense、curiosity、surprise。同じ三つを順序で定義する：結末の先送り／原因より先の結末／予期しない出来事。

**Ryan 2001, suspense の種類：** 「what suspense」（何が起こるか／彼らは何をするか）、「how (why) suspense」（どうしてこうなったのか）、「who suspense」（誰がやったのか）、「metasuspense」（作者はどう結びつけるのか）。[what ≈ arc; how ≈ process/reveal; who ≈ reveal; meta ≈ pattern、視聴者が作り手の craft に賭けている状態]

**Bordwell 1985, 仮説と gap の種類：** 仮説は curiosity か suspense；排他的か非排他的か；蓋然的か非蓋然的か；同時的か継起的か。gap は temporary か permanent か；diffuse（拡散）か focused（集中）か；flaunted（誇示）か suppressed（抑制）か。

**Carroll 1985/1990：** macro-question（映画全体の問い）対 micro-questions（場面レベル）。正確な語法としては UNVERIFIED；erotetic model 自体は確認済み。

**Tan 1996（本の章題より）：** interest は「thematic structures」と「character structures, empathy」によって維持される；そして F-emotions（fiction についての感情）対 A-emotions（artefact についての感情、つまり作りへの感嘆）。[A-emotions は「presence」/技能を見ることの、公刊されたなかで最も近い居場所だ]

**Kintsch 1980：** 「cognitive interest（認知的関心）」（予期しない情報）対「emotional interest（感情的関心）」（覚醒を呼ぶ話題）。reveal は前者、arc は後者だ。

**Dyer 1977, utopian sensibilities：** energy、abundance、intensity、transparency、community。[*ナンバー*が何を差し控えるかではなく何を届けるかについての、唯一の公刊された分類]

**Vonnegut / Reagan et al. 2016, arc の形：** 上昇；下降；下降–上昇（man in a hole）；上昇–下降（Icarus）；上昇–下降–上昇（Cinderella）；下降–上昇–下降（Oedipus）。**Booker 2004：** Overcoming the Monster; Rags to Riches; The Quest; Voyage and Return; Comedy; Tragedy; Rebirth.

arc / process / reveal / pattern に対応する四種類を持つ公刊された分類はない。繰り返し現れる区分は、*時間*についての二分（未来向きの suspense 対 過去向きの curiosity）に、撹乱要因としての surprise を加えたものだ。「process」はジャンルであって（Skvirsky）、interest の種類ではない。彼女の説明では、完成品がわかった時点で同じ三つが働く。「pattern」（次の beat は決まるか）には narratology 上の名前がなく、最も近いのは Ryan の「metasuspense」と Tan の A-emotions だ。

### 4. 物語なしで引きつける

**Spectacle（見世物）。** Gunning (1986, 1989)：初期映画は「exhibitionist」であり、「attraction」と「astonishment」で引きつけた。視聴者に呼びかけ、何かを見せる。plot は要らない。attraction は物語映画のなかにナンバー、スタント、効果として生き残っている。Bordwell（*Planet Hong Kong*, 2000）はアクションの仕組みを付け加える：「expressive amplification（表現的増幅）」と「pause-burst-pause（静–動–静）」のリズム。読み取れる身振りが区切られ、各 burst が準備されて決まる。これは arc ではなく pattern の hold だ。

**ナンバー。** Dyer (1977)：ナンバーは非表象的な手段で energy、abundance、intensity、transparency、community を届ける。視聴者は答えではなく*感情*のために残る。Altman (1987)：「無駄に見える」ナンバーこそが構造だ。どちらも、物語が止まっても hold は続くと言っている。

**Process（工程）。** Skvirsky (2020)：process genre は「absorption」によって引きつける。既知の完成品と未知の手順の組み合わせなので、誰も変化しないのに物語の形をしている（彼女はこれを「surprise, suspense, and curiosity」に結びつける）。Smucker の修正：引きつけるのは、手と道具の目に見える ingenuity だ。これは「process＝完成するか」を独自の種類として支持するが、人の代わりに製品を置いた suspense かもしれないという留保がつく。

**Performance / presence。** Fried (1980)：「absorption」（演者はあなたがいることを知らない）対「theatricality」（演者はあなたに語りかける）。Gunning の direct address は theatricality であり、作業中の職人を撮った映像は absorption だ。ダンス研究はこの hold を「kinesthetic empathy（運動感覚的共感）」と呼ぶ（John Martin, 1930s; Reynolds & Reason, eds., Intellect, 2012）：観客は動く者とともに動く。Tan の A-emotions（artefact とその作り手への感嘆）は、技能を見ることの映画理論上の居場所だ。これらのどれも開いた問いを必要としない。物語は必須ではないという、文献側の答えだ。

**Loops（ループ）。** Manovich, *The Language of New Media* (MIT Press, 2001) には "Loop as a Narrative Engine" という章があるが、受容理論は提示していない。この範囲のなかに ASMR やシームレスなループを説明するものはない。

**各系譜が一致する点。** 公刊されている hold の仕組みは二つある：(a) 情報についての開いた expectation（Sternberg, Carroll, Bordwell, Brewer & Lichtenstein, Baroni）；(b) 何も差し控えずに届けられる感覚（Gunning, Dyer, Fried, Martin）。Tan の「expectation of reward としての interest」は両方にまたがる唯一の用語だ。報酬は答えでも感情でもありうるからだ。
