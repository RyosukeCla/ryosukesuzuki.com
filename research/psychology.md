# What keeps a viewer watching: the psychology and neuroscience slice

Status: literature survey, 2026-09-16. Sibling surveys cover narratology/screenwriting and
media/platform research; this one stays on curiosity, expectation, interest, enjoyment and
attention over time. Every row was checked against a web search (year, venue, the author's own
term). Anything not checked is marked UNVERIFIED. No naming conclusion is drawn here.

## 1. Theories

| Author, year, work | Their term | What it explains | What it does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Berlyne 1960, *Conflict, Arousal, and Curiosity* (McGraw-Hill); 1966 "Curiosity and exploration", *Science* 153 | **collative variables** (novelty, uncertainty, conflict, complexity); **perceptual** vs **epistemic** curiosity; **specific** vs **diversive** exploration | Stimulus properties that raise arousal and trigger looking; the split between sensory puzzlement and wanting-to-know | Says little about *when* curiosity resolves; treats it as drive reduction, later disputed |
| Berlyne 1971, *Aesthetics and Psychobiology* (Appleton-Century-Crofts) | **arousal potential**, **hedonic value**, the **Wundt curve** (inverted U) | Why moderate novelty/complexity is liked and extremes are boring or aversive | Empirical support for the inverted U is mixed; fluency theory (below) explains the low end better |
| Loewenstein 1994, "The psychology of curiosity", *Psychological Bulletin* 116 | **information gap**; curiosity as "cognitively induced deprivation" | A salient gap between what you know and want to know is aversive and pulls attention until closed; peaks at partial knowledge | Only epistemic; does not cover perceptual or aesthetic holding, or liking without a gap |
| Kang et al. 2009, "The wick in the candle of learning", *Psychological Science* 20 | **epistemic curiosity**; inverted U on confidence | fMRI: curiosity for trivia scales with caudate (reward-anticipation) activity; highest at moderate confidence; better memory for surprising answers | Trivia questions, not moving images |
| Jepma et al. 2012, *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience* 6 | induction and **relief** of **perceptual curiosity** | Blurred images: induction activates insula/ACC (conflict, arousal); disambiguation activates striatum (reward). Curiosity is aversive, its termination rewarding | Static images |
| Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath 2014, *Neuron* 84 | **states of curiosity** | High-curiosity states enhance midbrain/nucleus accumbens activity and memory for both the target and incidental material | Not about duration of watching |
| Kidd & Hayden 2015, "The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity", *Neuron* 88 | (review) argues for a **functional** view over definitions and taxonomy | Sums up Berlyne, Loewenstein, Kang, Gruber; curiosity as information-seeking that pays off in learning | Explicitly declines to settle the taxonomy this survey is after |
| Marvin & Shohamy 2016, *JEP: General* 145 | **information prediction error** | Answers that satisfy more than the question promised are remembered better; information is treated as reward | Trivia |
| Noordewier & van Dijk 2017, "Curiosity and time", *Cognition and Emotion* 31 | **not knowing** vs **almost knowing**; **time-to-resolution** | Long time-to-resolution makes curiosity feel like deprivation; as resolution nears, anticipation and positive affect rise | Lab vignettes |
| Gottlieb & Oudeyer 2018, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 19 | **active sampling**; **expected information gain**; information sampling vs information search | Attention as a policy that samples where uncertainty reduction is expected | Computational; no account of enjoyment |
| Silvia 2005, "What is interesting?", *Emotion* 5; 2006, *Exploring the Psychology of Interest* (OUP); 2008, "Interest—the curious emotion", *Current Directions* 17 | **interest** as an emotion with two appraisals: **novelty–complexity** and **coping potential** (comprehensibility) | Something is interesting when new/complex *and* understandable; interest is distinct from happiness | Not time-structured: says what is interesting, not how interest is held or released |
| Izard 1977, *Human Emotions* (Plenum) | **interest-excitement**, a fundamental emotion, "the most ubiquitous" | Interest as the default motivational state of attention | No mechanism for its rise and fall |
| Hidi & Renninger 2006, *Educational Psychologist* 41 | **triggered situational interest → maintained situational interest → emerging individual interest → well-developed individual interest** | Interest as a developmental sequence; "triggered" vs "maintained" is the hook/hold distinction in interest research | Classroom scale, not seconds |

| Huron 2006, *Sweet Anticipation* (MIT) | **ITPRA** (Imagination, Tension, Prediction, Reaction, Appraisal); **contrastive valence**; **prediction response** | Pre-outcome tension plus post-outcome reward; pleasure at resolution scales with prior tension; correct prediction is itself rewarding | Built for music; "imagination" step assumes the outcome is representable |
| Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997, *Science* 275 | **reward prediction error** | Dopamine fires to unexpected reward, not to predicted reward; the neural basis of "better than expected" | Reward, not information, in the original |
| Friston 2010, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 11; Clark 2013, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 36 | **free energy** / **prediction error minimisation**; "prediction machines" | The brain as a hierarchy that predicts input and attends to error | Too general to say what a viewer waits for; pleasure is an add-on |
| Van de Cruys & Wagemans 2011, "Putting reward in art", *i-Perception* 2 | **prediction error** account of aesthetics | Reward comes from moving from uncertainty to predictability; artists build predictions then break them | Tentative, static art |
| Lehne & Koelsch 2015, *Frontiers in Psychology* 6 | **tension** and **suspense**, domain-general | Tension = conflict/instability/uncertainty plus predictive processes aimed at an emotionally significant future event; explicitly covers music, film, literature | Model, not a scale; little data of its own |

## 2. Published taxonomies of kinds

Verbatim categories; the mapping to the four working kinds is a note, not a claim.

- **Berlyne 1960**: *perceptual* vs *epistemic* curiosity; *specific* vs *diversive* exploration. (Specific = a particular question; diversive = seeking stimulation of any kind. Map: specific ≈ reveal/arc; diversive ≈ pattern/presence.)
- **Loewenstein 1994**: one kind, the *information gap*, with the "manageable gap" as the operative variable.
- **Litman 2005** (*Cognition and Emotion* 19), **2008** (*Personality and Individual Differences* 44): *Interest-type (I-type)* vs *Deprivation-type (D-type)* epistemic curiosity, mapped onto Berridge's *wanting/liking*. I-type = pleasure of discovering something new; D-type = need to obtain a specific missing answer. (Map: D-type ≈ reveal; I-type ≈ process/pattern.)
- **Kashdan et al. 2018** (*J. Research in Personality* 73): *Joyous Exploration, Deprivation Sensitivity, Stress Tolerance, Social Curiosity, Thrill Seeking*. Trait scale, but *Social Curiosity* is the only published curiosity dimension that names "what other people say and do" as its own object, which is close to *presence*.
- **Silvia 2005**: not kinds of interest but two *appraisals*: *novelty–complexity* and *coping potential*.
- **Hidi & Renninger 2006**: *triggered situational, maintained situational, emerging individual, well-developed individual* interest.
- **Pekrun et al. 2017** (*Cognition and Emotion* 31), Vogl et al. 2020 (*Emotion* 20): the *epistemic emotions*: *surprise, curiosity, enjoyment, confusion, anxiety, frustration, boredom*; surprise → curiosity → (confusion) is the observed trigger sequence.
- **Huron 2006**: five response systems, *Imagination, Tension, Prediction, Reaction, Appraisal*.
- **Noordewier & van Dijk 2017**: two phases of one episode, *not knowing* and *almost knowing*.
- **Berridge 2009**: *wanting, liking, learning*.
- **Brewer & Lichtenstein 1982** (*J. Pragmatics* 6, structural-affect theory): *suspense, curiosity, surprise*, defined by the order in which discourse reveals events. This belongs to the narratology sibling; noted because it is the only taxonomy that is time-ordered by the *telling*, which is what an editor controls.
- **Trost et al. 2017**: four *levels of entrainment*: *perceptual, autonomic physiological, motor, social*.
- **Grossnickle 2016** (*Educational Psychology Review* 28): review of 39 papers; curiosity definitions fall into four themes, most often *need for knowledge* and *motivator of exploration*; argues curiosity and interest are distinct but conflated in practice.

## 3. Candidate umbrella terms for "what the format makes the viewer wait for"

| Term | Evidence behind it | As a lesson word |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest** | Silvia 2005/2008, Izard 1977, Hidi & Renninger 2006: an emotion with a clear appraisal structure (new + graspable), the broadest and best-defended construct here. But it describes a *state*, not a thing waited for; a face can be interesting with nothing open | Natural, but too broad: "keep it interesting" is what creators already say and it names nothing to build |
| **Curiosity** | Loewenstein 1994, Kang 2009, Jepma 2012, Gruber 2014: aversive gap, rewarding closure, memory boost; Litman's I/D split gives it two kinds | Natural and already in creator vocabulary ("curiosity gap"); leans toward *reveal* and under-serves arc/process/pattern |
| **Expectation** | Meyer 1956, Huron 2006, predictive processing: the most general mechanism; every kind (arc, process, reveal, pattern) is a prediction awaiting confirmation or violation | Accurate but cold; "set an expectation" reads as instruction rather than craft |
| **Anticipation** | Huron's title word; Noordewier's *almost knowing*; Berridge's *wanting*; Kang's caudate signal is reward *anticipation* | Warm, forward-looking, covers process and pattern well; weaker for the retrospective *reveal* |
| **Tension** | Lehne & Koelsch 2015 (domain-general), Meyer, Huron's T, Lewin's systems under tension; already the curriculum's Cycle word | Creators know it; risk of collision with the Cycle layer since tension is the *feeling*, not the *thing awaited* |
| **Suspense** | Lehne & Koelsch treat it as tension about an emotionally significant future event; Brewer & Lichtenstein make it one of three | Reads as thriller-specific; poor fit for tutorials and satisfying loops |
| **Question / open question** | Loewenstein's gap, Berlyne's specific exploration; Sternberg-style "narrative interests" (sibling survey) | Very natural ("what question does the video ask?"), but presence and pattern have no question |
| **Promise** | Not a psychology term; it is Hidi & Renninger's *trigger* plus Noordewier's *time-to-resolution* stated from the maker's side | Already lesson 01's word; the literature offers no obstacle to extending it |
| **Wanting** | Berridge 2009, Litman 2005: the dissociation from liking is the cleanest evidence that "waiting" and "presence" are different systems | Too clinical for creators |

## 4. Holding without waiting

The literature does treat this as a separate mechanism, and the split is the same one from three directions:

- **Berridge**: *wanting* (dopaminergic, anticipatory, aversive when unmet) vs *liking* (hedonic, in the moment). Litman 2005 imports exactly this into curiosity: D-type curiosity is wanting; I-type is closer to liking.
- **Fluency** (Reber et al. 2004): pleasure from *ease* of processing, which is the opposite of the gap. Symmetry, repetition, prototypicality, high contrast. Bornstein's inverted U says repetition pleases until boredom. This is the best-supported account of "oddly satisfying": completion, symmetry, clean edges are fluency and Gestalt closure, not an open question.
- **Faces and people**: faces capture attention (Langton 2008, contested) and beautiful faces are rewarding to look at with no task (Aharon 2001). Parasocial interaction (Horton & Wohl) is a within-viewing sense of being addressed; Dibble et al. separate that from the relationship that brings viewers back. Warmth/competence (Fiske) is what the viewer is reading in the face.
- **Entrainment** (Large & Jones, Trost): attention locks to a beat and concentrates at predicted moments. This is a *prediction* mechanism with a period of milliseconds, so a loop or dance holds by expectation at the beat level with no arc at all. It sits between the two camps: it is expectation, but of the kind that is always immediately confirmed.
- **ASMR** (Barratt & Davis, Poerio): parasympathetic, "flow-like", triggered by personal attention and slow, crisp sensory detail; responders only.

What this suggests, without deciding names: the literature supports two systems, not one, and the four working kinds all live on the *wanting* side. "Presence" (a face, warmth, being addressed) and "satisfying" (fluency, closure, ASMR) are *liking*, held by ease and reward rather than by an open expectation. Entrainment is the bridge: a video can hold on expectation with a one-beat horizon and never open a gap. Whether "story is required" therefore has a literature answer: no, but *expectation* of some horizon (a beat, a next stroke, a next word) is present in every case surveyed except pure liking, and pure liking decays by boredom (Bornstein) faster than a gap does.

Open point the sources do not settle: no study measures second-by-second retention of short video against any of these constructs. Everything above is inferred from trivia, music, static images and long narratives.

### 2. 公刊された種類の分類

原文どおりのカテゴリ；4 つの作業上の種類への対応づけはメモであって主張ではない。

- **Berlyne 1960**：*perceptual* と *epistemic* の curiosity；*specific* と *diversive* の exploration。（specific = 特定の問い；diversive = 何であれ刺激を求めること。対応：specific ≈ reveal/arc；diversive ≈ pattern/presence。）
- **Loewenstein 1994**：一種類、*information gap*、操作変数としての「manageable gap（扱えるギャップ）」。
- **Litman 2005**（*Cognition and Emotion* 19）、**2008**（*Personality and Individual Differences* 44）：*Interest-type (I-type)* と *Deprivation-type (D-type)* の epistemic curiosity、Berridge の *wanting/liking* に対応づけられる。I-type = 新しいものを発見する快；D-type = 特定の欠けている答えを得たいという要求。（対応：D-type ≈ reveal；I-type ≈ process/pattern。）
- **Kashdan et al. 2018**（*J. Research in Personality* 73）：*Joyous Exploration, Deprivation Sensitivity, Stress Tolerance, Social Curiosity, Thrill Seeking*。特性尺度だが、*Social Curiosity* は「他人が何を言い、何をするか」をそれ自体の対象として挙げる唯一の公刊された curiosity 次元で、*presence* に近い。
- **Silvia 2005**：interest の種類ではなく二つの *appraisal*：*novelty–complexity* と *coping potential*。
- **Hidi & Renninger 2006**：*triggered situational, maintained situational, emerging individual, well-developed individual* の interest。
- **Pekrun et al. 2017**（*Cognition and Emotion* 31）、Vogl et al. 2020（*Emotion* 20）：*epistemic emotions*（認識的感情）：*surprise, curiosity, enjoyment, confusion, anxiety, frustration, boredom*；surprise → curiosity →（confusion）が観察されたトリガーの順序。
- **Huron 2006**：五つの反応系、*Imagination, Tension, Prediction, Reaction, Appraisal*。
- **Noordewier & van Dijk 2017**：一つのエピソードの二つの相、*not knowing* と *almost knowing*。
- **Berridge 2009**：*wanting, liking, learning*。
- **Brewer & Lichtenstein 1982**（*J. Pragmatics* 6、structural-affect theory）：*suspense, curiosity, surprise*、談話が出来事を明かす順序によって定義される。これは narratology の姉妹サーベイに属する；*語り*によって時間順に並ぶ唯一の分類であり、語りこそ編集者が制御するものなので記しておく。
- **Trost et al. 2017**：四つの *levels of entrainment*：*perceptual, autonomic physiological, motor, social*。
- **Grossnickle 2016**（*Educational Psychology Review* 28）：39 本の論文のレビュー；curiosity の定義は四つのテーマに分かれ、最も多いのは *need for knowledge* と *motivator of exploration*；curiosity と interest は別物だが、実際には混同されていると論じる。

### 3. 「フォーマットが視聴者に待たせるもの」を指す包括語の候補

| 用語 | 裏付けとなる証拠 | レッスンの語として |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest** | Silvia 2005/2008、Izard 1977、Hidi & Renninger 2006：明確な appraisal 構造（新しい＋把握できる）をもつ感情で、ここで最も広く、最もよく擁護された構成概念。ただし記述するのは*状態*であって、待たれるものではない；何も開いていなくても顔は interesting でありうる | 自然だが広すぎる：「keep it interesting」は作り手がすでに言っていることで、作るべきものを何も名指さない |
| **Curiosity** | Loewenstein 1994、Kang 2009、Jepma 2012、Gruber 2014：不快なギャップ、報酬となる閉合、記憶の増強；Litman の I/D の分割で二種類になる | 自然で、すでに作り手の語彙にある（「curiosity gap」）；*reveal* に傾き、arc/process/pattern には手が回らない |
| **Expectation** | Meyer 1956、Huron 2006、predictive processing：最も一般的な機構；あらゆる種類（arc、process、reveal、pattern）が、確認か違反を待つ予測である | 正確だが冷たい；「set an expectation」は技芸ではなく指示のように読める |
| **Anticipation** | Huron の書名の語；Noordewier の *almost knowing*；Berridge の *wanting*；Kang の caudate 信号は報酬の *anticipation* | 温かく前向きで、process と pattern をよく覆う；回顧的な *reveal* には弱い |
| **Tension** | Lehne & Koelsch 2015（領域一般）、Meyer、Huron の T、Lewin の systems under tension；すでにカリキュラムの Cycle の語 | 作り手が知っている；tension は*待たれるもの*ではなく*感情*なので、Cycle の層と衝突する恐れがある |
| **Suspense** | Lehne & Koelsch は、情動的に重要な未来の出来事についての tension として扱う；Brewer & Lichtenstein は三つのうちの一つとする | スリラー特有に読める；チュートリアルや satisfying なループには合わない |
| **Question / open question** | Loewenstein の gap、Berlyne の specific exploration；Sternberg 流の「narrative interests」（姉妹サーベイ） | とても自然（「この動画はどんな問いを立てるか？」）だが、presence と pattern には問いがない |
| **Promise** | 心理学の用語ではない；Hidi & Renninger の *trigger* と Noordewier の *time-to-resolution* を、作り手の側から言い直したもの | すでにレッスン 01 の語；文献はこれを拡張することに何の障害も示さない |
| **Wanting** | Berridge 2009、Litman 2005：liking との分離は、「待つこと」と「presence」が別の系であることの最も明快な証拠 | 作り手には臨床的すぎる |

### 4. 待たせずに引き留める

文献はこれを別の機構として扱っており、その分割は三つの方向から見て同じものだ：

- **Berridge**：*wanting*（dopamine 系、予期的、満たされないと不快）と *liking*（快楽的、その瞬間のもの）。Litman 2005 はまさにこれを curiosity に持ち込む：D-type の curiosity は wanting で、I-type は liking に近い。
- **Fluency**（Reber et al. 2004）：処理の*容易さ*から来る快で、ギャップの正反対。対称、反復、典型性、高コントラスト。Bornstein の逆 U 字は、反復は退屈になるまでは快いと言う。これが「oddly satisfying」の最もよく裏付けられた説明だ：完成、対称、きれいな縁は fluency と Gestalt の closure であって、開いた問いではない。
- **顔と人**：顔は注意を捕捉し（Langton 2008、争いあり）、美しい顔は課題なしでも見るだけで報酬になる（Aharon 2001）。parasocial interaction（Horton & Wohl）は視聴中に語りかけられている感覚；Dibble et al. はそれを、視聴者を呼び戻す relationship から切り分ける。warmth/competence（Fiske）は視聴者が顔から読み取っているものだ。
- **Entrainment**（Large & Jones、Trost）：注意はビートに同期し、予測された瞬間に集中する。これは周期がミリ秒の*予測*機構であり、ループやダンスは arc が一切なくてもビートの水準の expectation で引き留める。二つの陣営の間に位置する：expectation ではあるが、常に即座に確認される種類のものだ。
- **ASMR**（Barratt & Davis、Poerio）：副交感神経的、「flow に似た」、個人的な注意とゆっくりしたはっきりした感覚的ディテールで誘発される；反応する人のみ。

名前を決めずにここから言えること：文献は一つではなく二つの系を支持しており、4 つの作業上の種類はすべて *wanting* の側にある。「presence」（顔、温かさ、語りかけられること）と「satisfying」（fluency、closure、ASMR）は *liking* で、開いた expectation ではなく容易さと報酬によって保たれる。entrainment が橋になる：動画は一拍先の地平への expectation で引き留めることができ、ギャップを一切開かなくてよい。したがって「物語は必須か」には文献上の答えがある：否、ただし何らかの地平（一拍、次の一筆、次の一語）への *expectation* は、純粋な liking を除いてサーベイしたすべての事例に存在し、純粋な liking はギャップより速く退屈で減衰する（Bornstein）。

出典が決着をつけていない点：これらの構成概念のいずれに対しても、短い動画の秒単位の視聴維持を測った研究はない。上記はすべて、トリビア、音楽、静止画、長い物語からの推論だ。
