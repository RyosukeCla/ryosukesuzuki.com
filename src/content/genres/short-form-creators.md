A genre is a topic together with everything gathered around it: the needs of its people, the
formats they recognise, the conventions that signal it, the figures who define it, and the trends
moving through it. This page is that, for people who make short videos. It is a working page:
every part below is a column I keep filling as I watch, and nothing here is finished.

## Topic

Making short vertical videos (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) that people keep watching. The topic is not
"video editing" and not "growth" alone; it is the seam between them, editing in the service of
being watched.

## Needs

What people come to this genre for. Two needs, and most creators hold both:

- **Grow.** Views, followers, the algorithm. The urgent one; the one with money behind it.
- **Get better.** Cuts, captions, sound, the craft. The one people admit to less.

The genre's standing promise is "this will make your next video do better". Content that speaks
in growth words and delivers craft is what the genre keeps.

## Formats

The containers this audience already knows how to watch, each with the promise it runs on
(promise kinds: outcome, answer, beat, feeling, person).

| Format | Runs on | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Talking head | person (+ answer) | face to camera; the default explainer |
| Tutorial / how-to | outcome about a thing (+ person) | the steps finish |
| Before/after | answer | the after glimpsed, then the before, then the after |
| Storytime | outcome about a person | told to camera |
| POV | feeling (+ outcome) | camera as the viewer's eyes |
| GRWM (get ready with me) | person (+ feeling) | talk while doing |
| Unboxing / reveal | answer | what is it |
| Reaction | person (+ answer) | watch someone watch |
| Day in the life | outcome + person | a light arc, mostly company |
| Street interview | answer + person | strangers, one question |
| Satisfying / process | feeling + beat | cleaning, cutting, pouring |
| Dance / transition | beat (+ person) | the beat lands |
| Listicle ("3 mistakes") | answer | countable promise |
| Autopsy / breakdown | answer | why a video died, second by second |

## Conventions

What makes a video read as this genre in its first second.

- Captions on, large, word-timed; most viewing is muted.
- Punch-ins and zooms on the beat; a cut every 1–3 s in talking segments.
- A sound effect on the cut, on the reveal, on the text.
- Hook line spoken and written in the first second; "wait for it"; "nobody tells you this".
- Screen recordings of analytics as proof; the retention graph as an image people recognise.
- Vertical 9:16, face in the upper third, UI-safe zones respected.

## Figures

Who defines the genre, and what each is known for.

- **Colin & Samir** — the "Stop, Hook, Payoff" method for shorts; the creator-economy interview show.
- **Jenny Hoyos** — "Hook, Foreshadowing, Narrative, Ending with a twist"; 34-second target; 90%+ retention.
- **Paddy Galloway** — packaging, outliers, "contradiction click", turning an outlier into a repeatable format.
- **George Blackman** — scripting; "the audience needs to know at all times what they're waiting for next".
- **vidIQ** — retention, re-hook, the metric vocabulary.
- **MrBeast's production document** (2024 leak) — CTR / AVD / AVP, "re-engagement", "no dull moments", "satisfying payoff".
